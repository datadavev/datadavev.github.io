Innate Tendencies

Behavior driven by innate tendencies.

behavior
Author

Dave Vieglais

What are the fundamental dimensions of behavior?

People tend to prefer additive rather than subtractive changes (Adams et al. 2021).

People habitually seek order and consistency (Brotherton 2015). This desire for order may conflict with our preference for additive changes.

Humans are quite good at finding patterns. So much so, that we can find patterns where there are none (Friedman 2020). The tendency to find patterns where none exist can be exacerbated by a situation of disorder and conflict (Brotherton 2015).

Some people have a willingness to believe in unseen, intentional forces and an attraction to Maichean narratives1, Oliver and Wood 2014.

1 A tendency to look at things as having two sides that are opposed.

When overwhelmed with information we have a tendency to simplify by reliance on paranormal or supernatural beliefs. It is something of a coping mechanism.

Paranormal belief scale: A belief in ghosts or extra sensory perception. See Louisiana Tech University and Tobacyk 2004

Supernatural belief scale: A belief in angels or the Devil (Jong, Bluemke, and Halberstadt 2013; Bluemke et al. 2017).

Creating a to-do list can result in more mind-wandering, suggesting that cues related to personal goals and concerns can trigger mind-wandering in a habitual stimulus-response-like fashion (Zedelius, Gross, and Schooler 2018, pp366). However, Masicampo and Baumeister 2011 found that making a plan for addressing the unresolved goals (rather than merely listing them) can free us from this kind of habitual mind wandering. Instead of merely listing things to be done, make the effort to determine how and when they are to be done, that is, plan the outcome ready for execution.

In chaotic situations, a tendency to follow those appearing to be more in control. (Is this a Trump control factor - create chaos and appear to be on top of the situation)
